# The Verdant Compact — Lore

## The Waking

When the Undersun burst and the Year of Falling Light began, most of Kelvarrow looked up in terror. The old forests looked up in recognition.

Where Aethershards fell on living woodland, the trees did not burn or break around them. Roots found the buried songlight and coiled close, the way roots have always found water — and then they grew, and then they *woke*. The first to open its rings was an elder oak in the Vale of Anthem-Fallow, gold light kindling in its bark like a second grain. Within a season, whole groves were awake: forest-spirits older than any kingdom, blinking in the ruin of the world and asking, in slow voices of creak and leaf-hush, what had been done to the song.

The druid circles who had tended those woods knelt. The beast-kin herds and packs, orphaned by drowned plains and burning ranges, came to the gold-lit trees for shelter and stayed for purpose. Together — spirit, circle, and herd — they swore the Root-Oath on the first waking tree: *tend what fell, mend what broke, and let nothing planted be unplanted.* That oath is the Compact. It has never been written down. It does not need to be; it is grown into the rings of every signatory.

## What the Shards Are

The Compact holds the belief the other powers mock and cannot disprove: the Aethershards are **seeds**. The Undersun did not die — it went to seed, as every great tree does at the end of its season, scattering itself so something new could grow. A shard cast into a furnace is murder. A shard hoarded in the dark is worse — a seed starved on a shelf.

But a shard planted in living soil sends out *roots of light*. The Compact's grafters have learned to lead those roots across the broken edges of the world, splicing sundered coastlines with living sutures of trunk and vine. The greatest of these, the Greatgraft, already binds three landmasses. Compact culture is built on this work: patience measured in seasons, generosity measured in harvests, and a conviction — gentle, unbudging — that the war ends not when one side wins, but when the world knits.

## Oremma, the Undersown

The Compact's voice is **Oremma, the Undersown**. She was mortal once — the first druid of Anthem-Fallow, dying of shard-burn taken during the Year of Falling Light. She asked to be buried holding the seed that had killed her. The forest that came back up in spring was her: a towering spirit of heartwood and gold-ringed bark, the shard still glowing behind her ribs like a swallowed dawn.

Oremma is patient the way glaciers are patient, and funny the way grandmothers are funny — dry, sudden, and always at your expense. She remembers every seed she has ever planted and mourns each one the Dominion melts by name. She does not hate her enemies. She simply intends to outgrow them, and she has never once been wrong about what grows.

## Conflict Hooks

**Against the Pyre Dominion:** Dominion sappers have discovered that the Greatgraft's heartwood conducts shardlight, and have set the western root-span alight to melt the seed-shards out of it — feeding the living bridge, foot by burning foot, into the Great Crucible. To the Compact this is not war; it is vivisection. Oremma has done what she has never done before: ordered the forest to march *toward* the fire.

**Against the Abyssal Choir:** A planted shard's note deepens as it roots — and the Choir's listeners call that change *corruption*, a note going green, lost to the true order of the worldsong. Choir tide-cantors have begun raising the sea into the Compact's coastal orchards, drowning groves acre by acre to pry sprouted shards from the roots before they change beyond recall. The Compact answers by growing the orchards faster than the water rises. Neither side has blinked in three years.
