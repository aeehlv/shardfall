# Pyre Dominion — Lore

## Forged in the Wound

When the Undersun burst, nowhere broke deeper than the Cinderreach. The crust tore down to living magma, and the people who survived the Year of Falling Light did so in the one place nobody else dared follow them: the heat. They sheltered in cooling lava tubes, drank melt-water off ridges of new glass, and rediscovered bronze in the ruins of their own cities, because bronze was what the fires left them. Somewhere in that first ash-black winter, a smith named Ostrekka Vhal held a fallen Aethershard to the mouth of a lava-vein, watched its frozen songlight flare like iron coming to temper, and preached the sermon the Dominion still recites at every casting: *the world was not destroyed. It was struck.*

That is the whole of the Doctrine of the Unfinished Work. The Undersun was ore. The Shattering was the first hammerblow of a Smith too vast to see, and a single strike does not finish a blade. The work has therefore fallen to hands small enough to hold hammers: gather every shard, feed them to the fire, and finish forging the world into its final, burning shape.

From that doctrine grew an empire that thinks like a foundry. Rank is earned at the anvil before it is worn on the field; every legionnaire is a smith, and every smith a soldier. Foundry-priests read scripture in the sparks that rise off consecrated anvils, and no two sermons burn alike. The dead are not buried but cast — poured into bronze legion-plates riveted onto the walls of the Great Crucible, so that even the fallen stand watch over the work. Hesitation is the only heresy. Metal left waiting goes cold.

## The Shards as Ore

To the Dominion, an Aethershard is neither coin nor holy note. It is stock metal awaiting the melt. Hoarding one is theft from the forge; planting one is spitting on the anvil. Every recovered shard is carried up the Bronze Road in full procession and cast into the Great Crucible, the city-sized furnace at the empire's heart — and each casting is called *returning the note to the metal*, for the melt sings a little louder every time. When the last shard in Kelvarrow joins that chorus, the pour begins, and the world comes out of the mold whole, seamless, and edged.

## The First Hammer

The Dominion is led by **Varkha Cindral, First Hammer of the Great Crucible**. Born slag-caste, she spent her childhood sorting cooling waste beneath the foundry floors and climbed every rank of the torch-legions without once losing a breach. She is famously silent — she measures twice and strikes once, and her patience is not gentleness but the patience of a smith watching for the exact color of readiness. She despises hesitation more than she despises defeat, leads from the front of every assault, and carries *Refrain*, a colossal forge-hammer whose head was quenched in the Crucible's own melt: a standing promise of the pour to come. Her officers say the same thing her enemies do — you never hear Varkha coming, only landing.

## The Quarrel of Fire and Song

On the Scalded Shelf, where a shallow arm of the Sunken Sea covers a drowned shard-vein, the Dominion has raised lava-dikes to boil the water back and mine the vein dry. The Abyssal Choir hears every boiled trench as a verse being burned alive — and has begun answering with hymns that un-sing heat itself, freezing Dominion foundries mid-pour. Varkha's response was one order: double the dikes.

## The Quarrel of Fire and Root

The Greatgraft's newest root-line, the Emberwake Graft, is growing toward the Cinderreach and sealing the lava-veins that feed the Great Crucible — like a wound being closed. Torch-legions burn it back each season; it returns faster, grown around swallowed shards the Verdant Compact refuses to surrender. The Compact calls it healing. The Dominion calls it strangulation. Varkha calls it fuel.
