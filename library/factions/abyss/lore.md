# Abyssal Choir — Faction Lore

**Slug:** `abyss` · **Palette:** deep teal, violet bioluminescence · **Playstyle:** control, spells, card manipulation

## The Drowning That Did Not End

When the Sunken Sea poured into the wound of the Shattering, it took the old kingdom of the deeps — Nystrenne of the ten thousand bells — down with it, the bell-towers still ringing as they sank. Most of its people drowned. But the Year of Falling Light rained Aethershards into the sea as well as onto the land, and shards fall slowly through water, glowing all the way down. Those who sank beside them, lungs full of brine and eyes full of songlight, did not all die. The pressure remade them. The dark remade them. They rose from the trench floor changed — pale, many-eyed, patient — and in the crushing silence they heard what no surface ear can hear: the shards were still singing.

The first of the changed gathered in the drowned nave of Nystrenne's great cathedral and did the only thing their old faith had taught them. They sang back. And a shard answered.

## Culture and Creed

From that first answered note grew the Abyssal Choir: a drowned congregation arranged by iron liturgy into choirs of thirty-three voices — thirty-two to hold a hymn aloft, and one to end it. Among them speech is rationed and silence is reverence; a word spoken plainly is spent, but a word sung is invested. Their cathedral-trenches glow with their own violet and teal light, and their clergy spend lifetimes transcribing the **Score** — the slowly assembling map of the worldsong, note by recovered note.

The Choir's creed is this: the Shattering was not a death. It was an opening. The Undersun's first movement ended, as all movements end, and its second waits unperformed — but only if every surviving note is gathered, tuned, and sung in true order. Until that day, the Choir collects, and listens.

## The Shards

The Choir does not spend Aethershards, and does not exactly arm itself with them. It *archives* them. Each shard is one syllable of scripture, carried in lantern-reliquaries and woven into shard-hymns — harmonies that unmake spells mid-cast, rewrite memory, and call drowned possibilities up into the light. To destroy a shard is not theft or waste; it is deleting a word from the only copy of the only book. The Choir knows no deeper sin.

## The First Voice

The Choir is led by **Maelvyra, the First Voice** — a girl of Nystrenne who heard the Undersun's final chord as she drowned, and who was the first of the changed to sing back and be answered. She has since taken the Vow of the Borrowed Voice: she speaks only in notes recovered from shards, so every sentence she utters is sung, and every utterance is a small working. She is patient beyond mortal measure, courteous even to enemies, gentle as deep water — and exactly as impossible to argue with. She treats war as rehearsal and her foes as singers who have not yet learned their part. Nothing she does is hurried. Nothing she does is undone.

## The Argument, as the Choir Hears It

**Against the Pyre Dominion:** every shard cast into the Great Crucible is a note annihilated — a scripture-burning the second movement can never recover from. Now the Dominion has dredged up what the Choir calls **the Tonic**, the keystone note that sets the key of the entire second movement, and scheduled it for the melt. For the first time since the Shattering, whole choirs have surfaced. Coastal foundries have gone dark, their furnaces flooded, their garrisons found humming a tune none remember learning.

**Against the Verdant Compact:** a planted shard grows — and as it grows, its note bends. To Choir ears the Greatgraft is a continent-long dissonance grinding against the Score, every rooted note warping further from true with each ring of growth. Tide-cantors have begun singing loosening-hymns into the Greatgraft's underwater root-sutures, reclaiming buried notes before they detune beyond saving. The Compact's druids hear their bridge groan at low tide. If the Choir keeps singing, three landmasses come unstitched.
